For the First Time in Your Career, 50% Is a Credit

Fifty Percent

Most firms this size aren't losing money in ways anyone would notice day to day — until someone actually runs the numbers.

Most firms this size aren't losing money in ways anyone would notice day to day. No single mistake, no dramatic write-off — just free advice that was never billed, rework nobody flagged, referrals that quietly went cold. It adds up in the background, unseen, until someone actually runs the numbers.

Marcus had the calculator open on his second monitor for three days before he actually ran the numbers on his own firm.

Five professionals. $385 an hour, blended. He typed it in expecting a rounding error — a few thousand dollars, maybe, in free advice here and there. The total that came back was $399,517.

He did what any lawyer would do. He checked the maths. Then he checked it again.

Then he found the line about recovery — that getting back half of it, roughly $200,000, was realistic within a year. And something in him bristled at "half." Fifty percent. He'd spent twenty-two years building a practice on the idea that fifty percent was barely a pass. A mark you'd never put your name to. The kind of number a junior associate got quietly pulled aside about.

He sat with that discomfort for a minute before he saw it properly.

Nowhere else in his career had fifty percent ever meant $200,000 landing back in the business, recovered from money that was already gone, that he hadn't even known was leaving.

He wasn't being asked to be brilliant. He was being asked to close half a gap he didn't know existed a week earlier.

For the first time in his professional life, fifty percent wasn't a mark he'd be ashamed of. It was the best number he'd seen all year.
